## What BUSL-1.1 allows before the Change Date

Anyone may, without asking:

- read, copy and redistribute the code, in source or compiled form, original
  or modified;
- modify it and create derivative works;
- use it in **non-production** settings: development, testing, research,
  security auditing, evaluation, benchmarking, teaching, and running test
  networks or demonstrations.

Copies and derivative works stay under BUSL-1.1 until their Change Date, and
each copy must carry the license (`LICENSE-BUSL`).

**Production use is not granted.** The Additional Use Grant is `None`.
Running the BUSL-1.1 components in production, for example proving the
batches or results of a real election or running a sequencer that settles
real elections, requires a commercial license from Synergize S.L. until the
Change Date of the version you use.

BUSL-1.1 restricts *production use*, not commercial activity as such. The
license itself permits redistributing copies (even for a fee), commercial
security audits, paid development or consulting on the code, and other
non-production activities. Whoever puts a copy into production still needs a
production license. BUSL-1.1 does not define "production use"; if you are
unsure whether your use counts, ask (see below).

BUSL-1.1 is a **source-available** license. It is not an open source license
and is not approved by the Open Source Initiative. Each version becomes open
source (AGPL-3.0-or-later) on its Change Date.

## When each version becomes AGPL-3.0-or-later

Each published version has its own Change Date, three calendar years after
the day it was first published. On that date, the version becomes available
under the GNU Affero General Public License v3.0 or later, and the BUSL-1.1
restrictions end for that version. BUSL-1.1 also caps the restricted period
at four years from first publication, whichever comes first.

The Change Date of a version is stated in that version's `LICENSE-BUSL`.
Every version is recorded, append-only, in
[licensing/busl-versions.csv](licensing/busl-versions.csv):

| Version | First published (UTC) | Change Date (becomes AGPL-3.0-or-later) |
|---|---|---|
| *No BUSL-1.1 version has been published yet.* | | |

Revisions of `main` published between two releases are covered by the
`LICENSE-BUSL` they contain, which names the previous release and its Change
Date, so they convert no later than three years after their own publication.
A later release never moves an earlier version's Change Date.
[CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md#releasing) explains how the dates are
assigned and checked.

## Earlier versions remain AGPL-3.0-or-later

Every version published before the change to BUSL-1.1 was licensed under
AGPL-3.0-or-later, and that grant is not revoked. Those versions remain
available under AGPL-3.0-or-later to everyone: tags `v0.1.0` to `v0.5.0`,
and every revision of `main` up to commit
`22dbedff4e67a352c751acde7a3158f8685d6098` (see
[licensing/agpl-versions.txt](licensing/agpl-versions.txt)). Their git

## Using the SDKs from other projects

`rust-sdk` and `input-gen` are BUSL-1.1. A program that links them includes
BUSL-1.1 code and must respect BUSL-1.1. Do not link them into a program
you distribute under the AGPL or the GPL: those licenses do not allow the
BUSL-1.1 production restriction. The Go SDK stays AGPL-3.0-or-later.
